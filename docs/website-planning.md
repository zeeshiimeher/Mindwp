

# MindWP Money Pages Planning

This document is the working planning source for the MindWP money pages.

Purpose:

- keep the five-system offer clear,
- stop pages from becoming clones of each other,
- remove fake-looking proof from mockups,
- protect the service boundaries,
- give Claude/Codex a clean execution map.

This is not a copywriting dump. It is the decision document for what to protect, what to refine, what to rebuild, and what not to change.

Important: this document is a planning base, not fixed truth. When the live code, screenshots, business logic, or conversion strength contradict an earlier note, challenge the note and update the plan. Do not blindly follow this document if a section is weak, repetitive, risky, fake-looking, or commercially underpowered.

---

## 1. Core Strategic Decision

The five-system model stays.

Do **not** add a sixth public system.

The offer has expanded in depth because the connected handling layer can carry stronger capabilities such as missed-call recovery, conversation handling, appointment/consultation handling, quote/proposal follow-up, reminders, review handoffs, and owner visibility.

But this is not a new public system. It is deeper capability inside the existing five systems.

### Final Public Systems

1. **Smart Website Systems**  
   The visible front door and service/treatment page decision system.

2. **Local SEO Authority Systems**  
   Local search, listings, service areas, reviews, and local trust alignment.

3. **Lead Response & Handling Systems**  
   Missed calls, forms, messages, first replies, basic qualification, urgency, and first handoff.

4. **Follow-Up & CRM Systems**  
   Owner, status, next step, quote follow-up, appointment/consultation reminders, long-cycle enquiries, and review handoff.

5. **Reputation & Review Systems**  
   Review timing, feedback route, review monitoring, response ownership, and proof placement.

### Hard Guardrails

Do not create or reintroduce:

- Revenue Growth system,
- Revenue Recovery system,
- `/systems` route,
- public topic hubs,
- portfolio route,
- public GoHighLevel / HighLevel / GHL references,
- generic AI automation offer,
- AI chatbot vendor positioning,
- CRM reseller positioning,
- SEO ranking-hype offer,
- healthcare software / EMR / compliance positioning.

## 3. Page Ownership Boundaries

Each page should own one job.

| Page | Owns | Should not become |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Whole system story and review CTA | A full service-page explanation |
| Smart Website Systems | Website craft, page clarity, trust placement, context capture | Lead Response / CRM page |
| Local SEO Authority | Local discovery, listings, reviews, service areas, local page trust | Ranking/traffic SEO package |
| Lead Response & Handling | First reply, missed calls, messages, qualification, urgency, handoff | Follow-up CRM / chatbot product |
| Follow-Up & CRM | Owner/status/next-step, quote/booking/consultation follow-up | Missed-call response page / CRM software install |
| Reputation & Review | Completed work → feedback/reviews/proof placement | Review automation / review manipulation page |

Boundary rules:

- Homepage introduces; system pages go deeper.
- SWS builds the page and context surface.
- Local SEO aligns local discovery and local trust.
- Lead Response clarifies and hands off the first enquiry.
- Follow-Up CRM owns the next step after the first reply.
- Reputation turns completed work into visible proof.

The site needs realistic mockups, but not fabricated proof.

### Rule

Use realistic **formats**, not fake evidence.

Mockups should look real in structure, not fake in proof.

### Allowed in Mockups

Use generic but realistic labels:

- Recent verified reviews
- Verified review
- Review snippet placed near the decision point
- Local area covered
- Practice team
- Quote owner
- Service area
- Direct line

- Review request ready
- Follow-up due

### Avoid in Mockups

Remove or reduce:

- fake phone numbers,
- fake ratings like `4.9`,
- fake review counts like `312 reviews`,

- fake owner/customer names where not needed,

## 6. Cross-Page UI and Offer Audit

### Overall Verdict

No full-page rebuilds are currently planned, but this is not a restriction.

The pages are directionally strong and feel like one premium system brand instead of disconnected service pages. However, individual sections may still require partial rebuilds, full rebuilds, changed meaning, added sections, or removal if the source-code audit shows that the current section is weak, repetitive, risky, or not selling clearly enough.

The next work is:

1. realism and fake-proof cleanup,
2. copy safety,
3. capability clarity where needed,
4. CTA differentiation,
5. cross-page visual rhythm control,
6. final source cleanup.

### Current Page-Level Actions

| Page | Current status | Action level |
|---|---|---|
| Homepage | Strong full-system entry point | Realism audit + alignment copy |
| Smart Website Systems | Strong flagship website page | Copy/realism audit |
| Local SEO Authority | Strong local trust page | Copy polish + scenario refinement |
| Lead Response & Handling | Strongest first-response capability page | Capability + safety polish |
| Follow-Up & CRM | Strong after-first-response ownership page | Capability clarity + realism audit |
| Reputation & Review | Strong ethics-led reputation page | Ethics/copy polish |

### Cross-Page Visual Repetition Risk

The repeated rhythm is:

> dark hero → light explanatory sections → rounded cards → scope grid → fit/not-fit → FAQ → dark CTA

This consistency is good for brand cohesion, but can feel templated if not controlled.

Most repeated patterns:

- Fit / Not Fit sections,
- FAQ sections,
- final CTA sections,
- scope grid sections,
- “Where this connects” sections,
- large rounded white panel compositions.

### Recommendation

Do not redesign all repeated sections.

Instead:

1. keep structure consistent,
2. make final CTA visuals/copy distinct per page,
3. shrink or simplify “Where this connects” where not essential,
4. ensure each page has one unique signature section.


### Section Decision Levels

Use these labels when auditing or prompting work. Do not default to the gentlest label if the section needs stronger intervention.

| Decision | Meaning | Execution impact |
|---|---|---|
| Protect | Section is strategically and visually strong. Only tiny safety fixes allowed. | Minimal copy/data edit only |
| Light improve | Concept and layout are good. Needs copy, labels, fake-proof, or safety polish. | Copy/data edit only |
| Partial improve | Concept is good, but content, visual details, hierarchy, or mockup data need meaningful changes. | Copy/data + limited JSX/layout adjustment |
| Full rebuild | Current section purpose is right, but the section does not sell, differentiate, or communicate strongly enough. | Rebuild section JSX/content around same purpose |
| Replace meaning | Current section purpose is wrong, repetitive, or weak. Replace with a stronger section idea. | New strategy + new section build |
| Add section | A critical buyer recognition, capability, trust, or conversion moment is missing. | Add new section |
| Remove section | Section creates clutter, repeats another page, weakens rhythm, or adds no commercial value. | Delete section and adjust flow |

A page can remain “no full-page rebuild” while still having one or more sections rebuilt or replaced.

## 7. Execution Copy Guardrails

Use these rules when refining any page or creating Claude/Codex prompts from this document.

### Challenge the Plan Before Editing

Before creating an execution prompt, compare the current section against:

- the live source code,
- the latest screenshot/PDF,
- the page’s assigned system ownership,
- the adjacent pages,
- buyer recognition strength,
- commercial usefulness,
- fake-proof and safety risk,
- visual repetition across the site.

If the section is visually polished but commercially weak, upgrade the decision. If the section is strategically correct but visually repetitive, change the visual pattern. If the section is safe but too defensive, rewrite the angle. If the section repeats another page, change its meaning or reduce it.

### Business Reality First

Every section should start from the visible working-day reality before naming the system.

Use real buyer/business moments:

- calls,
- missed calls,
- forms,
- messages,
- quotes,
- bookings,
- consultation requests,
- local search checks,
- review moments,
- staff handoffs,
- owner memory,
- proof placement.

Do not lead with:

- platform,
- automation,
- dashboard,
- integration,
- feature,
- software,
- abstract “system” language before the buyer recognises the problem.

### FLOW → HANDLING → RESULT

Rewrite and refine sections using this sequence:

1. **FLOW** — what is happening in the buyer’s world.
2. **HANDLING** — how MindWP structures, clarifies, routes, places, or connects it.
3. **RESULT** — what becomes easier to see, handle, trust, or act on.

Avoid jumping straight from pain to feature.

### Protect Strong Authority Lines

Do not rewrite strong authority lines just to make them smoother.

Protect these unless the specific section plan says they are risky or weak:

- `Work Comes In. Too Much Slips Away.`
- `A better website is not just a better-looking page. It carries the decision.`
- `People Search Nearby. You Still Miss The Click.`
- `The Enquiry Arrived. Nobody Was There To Answer It.`
- `The Enquiry Was Answered. The Next Step Still Disappeared.`
- `Good Work Happens. The Proof Never Shows Up.`
- `A first reply that reads like the business — not a bot.`
- `Clarity, not analytics.`
- `No blasting. No gating. No shortcuts.`

### Public vs Internal Language

Use public buyer-facing language:

- calls,
- forms,
- messages,
- quotes,
- bookings,
- consultation requests,
- local trust,
- service area,
- reviews,
- proof,
- owner,
- status,
- next step,
- handoff,
- response path,
- review path.

Avoid leading with internal/tool language:

- infrastructure,
- backend,
- platform,
- automation engine,
- workflow,
- trigger,
- pipeline,
- opportunity,
- lifecycle,
- dashboard,
- integration,
- HighLevel,
- GoHighLevel,
- GHL.

### Clinic and Healthcare Safety

For specialist clinic copy, stay around:

- patient trust,
- service/treatment clarity,
- consultation requests,
- booking confidence,
- practice communication,
- pre-visit context,
- response,
- follow-up,
- reviews,
- proof.

Do not drift into:

- treatment outcomes,
- clinical decision-making by the system,
- medical claims,
- compliance claims,
- EMR,
- healthcare software,
- hospital operations,
- urgent treatment promises.

Use safer language:

- `urgent appointment request`,
- `time-sensitive practice enquiry`,
- `consultation request`,
- `practice team`,
- `pre-visit context`,
- `patient enquiry`,
- `appointment experience`,
- `communication experience`.

### Implementation Services Are Pathways, Not Systems

WordPress, Elementor, Bricks, Divi, WooCommerce, and redesign/rebuild are implementation pathways under **Smart Website Systems**.

They should support:

- clarity,
- trust,
- enquiry capture,
- maintainability,
- service/treatment page quality,
- connected handling.

They must not become generic builder-service offers or separate public systems.

### Adjacent-System Bridge Rules

Each page may reference adjacent systems, but only from its own role.

- SWS may mention response/follow-up after enquiry capture, but must not become Lead Response or CRM.
- Local SEO may mention reviews as trust signals, but must not become Reputation.
- Lead Response may mention follow-up ownership after routing, but must not become Follow-Up CRM.
- Follow-Up CRM may mention completed work triggering review requests, but must not become Reputation.
- Reputation may mention local trust, but must not become Local SEO.
- Homepage may introduce all systems, but must not explain every system in full.

### Scenario Is Not Proof

Scenario sections are illustrative unless backed by real evidence.

Do not use fictional examples as if they are:

- real client proof,
- case studies,
- testimonials,
- results,
- ROI,
- before/after performance evidence.

Avoid words like:

- `real client`,
- `real result`,
- `proven`,
- `testimonial`,
- `case study`,
- `ROI`,
- `X% increase`,
- `client result`,
- `each one was real`.

Use illustrative framing where needed:

- `Illustrative scenario`,
- `Common moment`,
- `Example path`,
- `Sample trust placement`,
- `Typical proof-loss moment`.

### Final Validation Checklist

Before finishing any batch, confirm:

1. No fake proof, fake numbers, fake testimonials, fake phone numbers, or fake ratings.
2. No public GoHighLevel / HighLevel / GHL references.
3. No AI/chatbot/vendor positioning.
4. No CRM software-first positioning.
5. No ranking, revenue, review, conversion, or response-time guarantees.
6. No treatment outcome or healthcare compliance claims.
7. Each page still owns its assigned system.
8. CTAs stay diagnostic.
9. Strong authority lines were protected unless the plan explicitly said to change them.
10. Build passes.
