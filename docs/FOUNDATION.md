# FOUNDATION — MindWP

Identity, buyer truth, and positioning. Read this first when a task touches strategy, copy, page intent, or design direction.

Foundation owns *who MindWP is and who it serves*. It does not own the commercial plan ([STRATEGY.md](./STRATEGY.md)), the offer structure ([OFFER-ARCHITECTURE.md](./OFFER-ARCHITECTURE.md)), the look ([DESIGN.md](./DESIGN.md)), page roles ([PAGES.md](./PAGES.md)), or voice ([WRITING.md](./WRITING.md)).

## Core identity

MindWP builds **smart websites** — conversion-focused websites with lead handling built in — for established service businesses and specialist clinics, sold globally across English-speaking markets. It is run by a builder shipping WordPress sites since 2015, with a small senior team.

Two parts, always together:

- **The website** makes the offer clear, builds trust, and captures enquiries (calls, forms, quotes, bookings, consultation requests).
- **The connected handling** is the response, ownership, follow-up, and review path *built into the same site* — so work doesn't slip away between being found, contacted, answered, followed up, and proven.

MindWP is not "website design." It's the website **and** the handling around it. That bundle is the product, and it's the reason a buyer chooses MindWP over any other web designer. The concrete offer is defined in [STRATEGY.md](./STRATEGY.md#what-we-actually-sell-the-concrete-offer).

MindWP should feel practical, operational, calm, premium, human, commercially serious, and grounded in real work.

## What MindWP is not

Not a generic web design agency, a cheap WordPress package shop, a Fiverr gig, a SaaS product, an AI chatbot vendor, an automation-bro offer, a CRM/tool reseller, a backend-platform reseller, an SEO ranking-hype provider, a generic doctor-marketing agency, a healthcare software/EMR/compliance company, or a portfolio-only design shop.

MindWP sells a website system that brings work in and makes sure it doesn't leak. It does not sell tools as the product, and it never names the backend platform publicly.

## Buyer truth

The buyer is an established service-business owner, operator, clinic owner, or practice manager. Their business already has activity — calls, searches, site visits, forms, quotes, consultations, jobs, appointments, reviews, staff pressure, repeat work, scattered inboxes, missed follow-up.

**The problem is rarely lack of demand. It's weak handling between moments.** Work slips when nearby customers can't find or verify the business, service/treatment pages don't build trust, calls are missed, forms land nowhere useful, response is slow, nobody owns follow-up, reviews aren't requested, and good work never becomes proof.

Public copy should make the buyer feel: *"This is exactly what happens here."* Then show the smart website that fixes it. Recognition first, then the concrete offer — not philosophy for its own sake. (The old site lingered too long in the philosophy; the buyer should reach the offer and real proof quickly.)

Good-fit buyers have meaningful job, case, or patient value; real activity; and visible leakage around enquiries, response, follow-up, and proof. MindWP is not for brand-new businesses with no traction, cheapest-website shoppers, brochure-only redesigns, or hype-seekers.

## Authority comes from real work

MindWP is a brand buyers don't know yet, so it earns trust with **evidence, not adjectives**: demonstration builds shown big, anonymised real client work, a real builder with a decade of craft, and a clear process. It never invents proof — no fake ratings, testimonials, metrics, rankings, or guarantees. The proof model is defined in [STRATEGY.md](./STRATEGY.md#proof-model-the-biggest-lever). This is the single biggest thing the previous site lacked.

## Website as the public control point

For most service businesses and small or growing clinics, the website is the front door and the visible place where several business moments meet: search/visibility, trust/proof, service or treatment clarity, enquiry capture, response and routing, follow-up ownership, and reviews. It's also usually where the buyer can most easily *see* that the wider handling path is weak.

This is why **Smart Website Systems is the public anchor** without swallowing the other four active systems. For specialist clinics the website is the **practice front door**: it helps patients understand the treatment, trust the provider, choose the next step, and book or enquire — never framed as hospital software, EMR, compliance, or treatment claims.

## Public buyer path

The plain-language mental model the buyer should grasp before any offer architecture:

1. Customers or patients **find** the business.
2. They **check** whether they trust it.
3. The website makes the **offer clear**.
4. They **contact / book / enquire**.
5. The enquiry **lands somewhere useful**.
6. **Response** happens quickly.
7. **Follow-up and ownership** stay visible.
8. Good work becomes **proof, reviews, and repeat value**.

This is the mental model, not a route taxonomy.

## Vocabulary boundaries

Lead public copy with working-day objects: calls, forms, quotes, consultations, bookings, jobs, appointments, reviews, staff pressure, missed replies, local trust, patient trust, proof, response, status, next step. Do **not** lead with infrastructure, backend tools, automations, platform names, dashboards, or internal taxonomy.

"System" is allowed — it's central to MindWP — but earn it with the problem first. "Connected handling" follows the same rule: show the calls/forms/follow-up gaps the buyer recognises, *then* name the path. Detailed language rules: [WRITING.md](./WRITING.md).

## How we build (Figma-first)

We design the **whole site in Figma first**, page by page, until the design is approved — then build the code. The design loop and the page-rebuild process live in [WORKFLOW.md](./WORKFLOW.md). When designing any page, answer: what's happening in the buyer's business, what they already recognise, where work slips, which active system owns it, what real proof backs it, and what the next step is. If those are unclear, read the relevant doc before inventing structure.
