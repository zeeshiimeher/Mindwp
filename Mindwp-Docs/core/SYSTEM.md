# 1. CORE IDENTITY

## 1.1 What MindWP Is

MindWP is a systems-first digital infrastructure consultancy for established service businesses.

We do not sell pages. We do not sell tools. We do not sell traffic. We do not sell hype.

We design and implement connected digital systems that improve:
- Enquiry handling
- Follow-up reliability
- Operational clarity
- Conversion efficiency
- Internal visibility

We prepare businesses for scale. We do not sell scale.

## 1.2 Category

**Company Category:** Systems-first digital infrastructure consultancy for service businesses.
**Flagship Framework:** Smart Website Systems.

The category explains what MindWP is. The flagship framework explains how it is most clearly understood and sold.

We do NOT: run paid ads as core service, guarantee rankings, promise lead volume, compete as design-only studio, position as SaaS, offer random digital services.

## 1.3 Strategic Positioning

Digital Infrastructure & System Implementation for Service Businesses.

We sit before growth agencies. Growth agencies increase traffic. We ensure businesses can handle traffic properly. Infrastructure before growth. Structure before scale.

SEO amplifies clarity. SEO cannot fix confusion.

## 1.4 Tone

Calm. Clear. Confident. Professional. Measured.

We sell reassurance, not excitement. Clients should feel calm and confident.

We never say: "skyrocket your leads", "guaranteed rankings", "affordable packages", "explosive growth", "dominate your competitors".

Anti-hype vocabulary (banned): Dominate, Explode, Disrupt, Revolutionary, Guaranteed, Hyper-growth, Skyrocket.

## 1.5 Target Market

**We serve:** Established service businesses. Average sale ≥ $300, lifetime value ≥ $1,000. Businesses already investing in visibility. Owners who value operational clarity.

**We avoid:** Budget-first buyers, page-count shoppers, guarantee seekers, hobby businesses, procurement-heavy enterprises.

**Front-door industry focus:**
- Phase 1 primary: Home services (Roofing, HVAC, Plumbing, Electrical, Landscaping)
- Phase 1 secondary: Beauty & Personal Care (Aesthetic clinics, Hair/salon, Lash/brow)

Home services receives primary promotional weight. Industry focus must never replace Smart Website gravity. Other qualified service businesses may be served where fit is strong.

## 1.6 Qualification & Filtering

**Strong fit:** Discusses operational pain, asks about follow-up timing, cares about tracking, wants structure, already investing in visibility.

**Weak fit:** "How much for 5 pages?", price-only negotiation, guarantee demands, freelancer comparison.

Exit calmly: "It sounds like you may be looking for a different type of service. We focus on infrastructure implementation."

**Decision filter:** Diagnose before prescribing. Structure before selling. Silence before discounting. Authority before persuasion. Long-term positioning over short-term revenue.

**Final test:** "How does this improve operational clarity?" If unclear → rewrite.

## 1.7 Pricing

No broad homepage pricing as primary conversion device. Target range: $1,500–$2,500 depending on scope.

Priced by implementation depth — not page count, plugin count, or design hours. No defensive discounting. Never defend price — reframe scope.

Homepage must not display page-count pricing, compare against freelancers, or justify pricing defensively.

## 1.8 Sales Discipline

We diagnose infrastructure gaps. We do not "sell websites." We do not push urgency. We build calm authority.

**Sales call:** Context Discovery → Infrastructure Diagnosis → Reframing → Solution Alignment → Pricing.

**Proposal order:** Operational Summary → Structural Recommendation → Scope Outline → Investment. Proposals must not read like marketing brochures, list plugin stacks, overload features, or justify pricing emotionally.

## 1.9 Proof Model

Until real case studies exist in sufficient number:
- Primary proof: system logic, architecture reasoning, process clarity, operational diagnosis
- Secondary proof: clearly labeled implementation scenarios

Never use fabricated testimonials, invented logos, fake statistics, or unlabeled fictional case studies.

# 2. SERVICE MAP

## 2.1 The Four Core Services

MindWP has four core services. Each one does one job. Every page, blog post, and case study must know which service it belongs to and stay inside that boundary.

### Smart Website

**What it does:** Builds a website that guides visitors toward getting in touch and captures their details properly.
**Role in the system:** Foundation layer. Everything else depends on this working first.
**The problem it solves:** People visit the website, look around, and leave. Nobody gets in touch. Nobody knows they were there.

**What it does NOT do:**
- Does not handle what happens after someone gets in touch (that's CRM)
- Does not get people to the website (that's SEO)
- Does not collect reviews (that's Reputation)

### CRM & Follow-Up

**What it does:** Tracks every enquiry, assigns it to someone, and makes sure follow-up actually happens.
**Role in the system:** Handles enquiries after they come in. Picks up where the website leaves off.
**The problem it solves:** Leads come in from calls, emails, and forms — some get missed, nobody knows who's handling what, and follow-up only happens when someone remembers.

**What it does NOT do:**
- Does not bring visitors to the website (that's SEO)
- Does not design or build the website (that's Smart Website)
- Does not collect reviews (that's Reputation)

### Local SEO

**What it does:** Makes the business visible on Google when people nearby search for the services they offer.
**Role in the system:** Brings people to the website. Feeds the top of the funnel.
**The problem it solves:** People are searching for exactly what this business does, but they're finding competitors instead.

**What it does NOT do:**
- Does not guarantee leads or conversions (that's the website's job)
- Does not handle enquiries (that's CRM)
- Does not collect reviews (that's Reputation)

### Reputation (Reviews)

**What it does:** Gets more genuine reviews from happy customers and catches unhappy ones before they post publicly.
**Role in the system:** Builds trust after the service is delivered. Supports SEO indirectly.
**The problem it solves:** The business does great work, but their Google reviews don't reflect it. Competitors with worse work have more reviews.

**What it does NOT do:**
- Does not drive traffic to the website (that's SEO)
- Does not capture or track leads (that's CRM)
- Does not change how the website looks or works (that's Smart Website)

## 2.2 Service Tiers (Gravity Model)

| Layer | Role | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Core Revenue Systems** | Strategic entry points | Smart Website (flagship), Local Authority & SEO, AI Lead Handling, Reputation & Review, Revenue Growth, CRM & Automation |
| **Tier 2 — Specialized Modules** | Solve specific operational pains | Booking & Scheduling, Missed Call Recovery, Review Automation, Lead Reactivation, Conversion Funnel, Unified Communication |
| **Tier 3 — Implementation** | Execution-layer entry pathways | WordPress Development, Website Redesign, CRM Implementation, Marketing Automation Setup, Funnel & Landing Page Dev, System Migration |

**Gravity rule:** Smart Website Systems is the conceptual framework. All other Tier 1 systems integrate into it. They must never override its dominance in hierarchy, navigation, or homepage composition.

**SEO exception:** Local Authority & SEO is the only Tier 1 system with controlled direct-intent acquisition — may lead when visitor intent is explicitly SEO-led, but must always reinforce website infrastructure context.

No horizontal equal-weight presentation. Smart Website must retain structural dominance.

```
SEO → Website → CRM → Reputation
```

1. **SEO** brings people to the website
2. **Website** guides them to get in touch
3. **CRM** tracks the enquiry and handles follow-up

# 4. PAGE BOUNDARIES

Every page must stay in its lane. If a sentence belongs to another service, remove it.

## Smart Website page boundaries

**Talks about:** How visitors behave on the site, enquiry capture (forms, calls, chat), what happens when someone lands on the site, page structure, clarity, navigation.

**Must NOT talk about:** How SEO rankings work, CRM logic, follow-up sequences, lead tracking, review collection.

## CRM page boundaries

**Talks about:** What happens after an enquiry comes in, lead tracking, assignment, follow-up, visibility into where every lead stands, automated reminders and sequences.

**Must NOT talk about:** Website design or page structure, SEO rankings, review collection.

## Local SEO page boundaries

**Talks about:** Being found on Google, search visibility for local services, Google Business Profile, directories, service pages, how Google reads the business.

**Must NOT talk about:** Conversion rates or enquiry capture, lead tracking or follow-up, review collection.

## Reputation page boundaries

**Talks about:** Getting reviews from happy customers, catching unhappy customers before they post publicly, timing of review requests, building trust through social proof.

**Must NOT talk about:** Website traffic, SEO rankings, lead tracking or CRM logic.

## The boundary test

Read every sentence. Ask: "Does this describe what THIS service does — or what another service does?"

If it describes another service → delete or move it. If it mentions another service's outcome as context, one line maximum.

# 6. BRIDGE RULES

The four services work together. But "everything is connected" is banned — it says nothing.

## How to reference other services

Use a single-line bridge. State what happens, not how it works.

**On Website, referencing SEO:**
✅ "Once people find your site — through Google, a referral, or a directory — the site takes over."

**On CRM, referencing Website:**
✅ "When someone fills in a form on your site, their details land in your CRM."

**On Reputation, referencing CRM:**
✅ "After a job is marked complete, the customer gets a review request."

**On SEO, referencing Website:**
✅ "Google reads your site to understand what you do and where you do it."

## Rules

1. Maximum one bridge sentence per section
2. Bridges describe the outcome, never the method
3. Bridges never use the other service's banned vocabulary
4. More than one sentence about another service = you've crossed the boundary

# 7. TOOL POSITIONING

MindWP is not a software company. We set things up for people. Tools are implementation details — never selling points.

**Rule:** Never lead with a tool name. Never position a tool as the product.

**How:** Write the sentence without the tool name. If the reader doesn't need the tool name → leave it out. If they do → add in parentheses.

✅ "Follow-up emails go out on their own — you don't have to send them."
❌ "We use GoHighLevel to automate your follow-ups."

**Tool names acceptable in:** "what's included" lists, FAQ answers, internal documentation. Never in heroes, headlines, card titles, or CTAs.

## Platform-Neutral Positioning

Smart Website Infrastructure must remain platform-agnostic in perception. Implementation platforms are delivery mechanisms — not category anchors.

- Platform names must not appear in hero headlines or define service identity
- Platform capability must not dominate value messaging
- Platform references live in implementation or technology sections only

If Smart Website feels like a "WordPress service" → positioning has drifted.

## Implementation Compatibility

Current primary operational platform: GoHighLevel (GHL). Provides backbone for CRM pipelines, workflow automation, lead routing, review automation, booking, and AI conversation handling.

- Service pages must never promise functionality outside the current stack
- Public messaging must remain platform-neutral
- Perception: Infrastructure first. Systems second. Platform third.

# 9. DRIFT PREVENTION

## What constitutes drift

- A doc restates rules owned by another doc
- A page uses vocabulary from the wrong service
- A page explains how another service works
- A tool name appears in a headline, hero, or CTA
- A supporting system appears equal to Smart Website
- Content creates terminology not in canonical.ts

## How to detect drift

1. **Role check:** Does this page stay in its service boundary? (§2, §4)
2. **Vocabulary check:** Does every sentence use this service's approved language? (§5)
3. **Boundary check:** Does it explain another service?
4. **Tool check:** Tool name in headline, hero, or CTA? (§7)
5. **Positioning check:** Can a customer clearly answer "what does this one service do?"

## Conflict resolution

| Scenario | Resolution |
|---|---|
| Doc X conflicts with SYSTEM.md | SYSTEM.md wins. Fix Doc X. |
| Doc X restates rules from another doc | Remove restatement. Add pointer. |
| Code conflicts with docs | Docs win. Fix the code. |
| Two non-root docs conflict | Check ownership table (§8). The designated owner wins. |
